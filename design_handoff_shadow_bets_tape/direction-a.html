Direction A
Tape — dense instrument
One continuous ledger, no cards. Every pixel of vertical space is a row you can read or act on. Tier stops being a badge and becomes a tick in the left gutter, so the only two numbers competing in a row are unit size and price — and unit wins. Build and watch are the same tape; locking changes what the columns mean rather than where you are.
Palette
#0A0B0D ink · #15181C panel · #4C9AFF my bet · #FF8A3D fade · #34C77B win · #E5484D loss
Type
Display & body IBM Plex Sans 500/600. Numeric IBM Plex Mono, tabular — negative American odds and 0.46u decimals stay aligned at 11px.
Layout
Full-bleed 46px rows on a 3px tier gutter. Hairlines instead of cards; game headers are dividers, not containers. Zero radius except controls.
Signature
The rule bar. A tier ruler with a threshold handle at the top of the list: slide it, the count and unit consequence update, then one press commits the whole set.

Optimizes for

Volume. 45 plays across five leagues is a lot of rows, and this reads roughly 9 markets per screen without scrolling. The rule bar turns a 45-tap morning into two gestures. Tier lives in the gutter, so unit size is unambiguously the loudest thing in every row.

Gives up

Warmth and forgiveness. Hairline rows have no per-game affordance to grab, taps are precise rather than generous, and a mis-tap on the tri-state control is easy. It also asks the user to learn the gutter tick as a tier encoding — legible once taught, invisible before that.

Desktop

The tape stays 520px wide as a left column and the right side becomes a detail pane: select a game, get every market, model reasoning, and line movement. The rule bar moves into a persistent left sidebar with league counts, so nothing has to scroll away.

Logos

Chips are 16–22px monogram placeholders — real team marks drop into the same 22px slot with no layout change. They carry team color at 22px better than a shrunk logo would, so the chip may be worth keeping as the fallback for leagues without clean assets.
